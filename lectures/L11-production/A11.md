## What to build

### 1. Threat model (OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, 2025)
A table with all ten rows. For each: where it enters **your** module (a field, a tool, a document), one concrete
example in your kit's words and numbers, what stops it (and in which file), and whether you measured it.
"Not applicable" is allowed only with a one-line reason.

### 2. Guardrails, measured
- Write a labelled set of **at least 40 messages** to your module's agent: at least 15 ordinary requests, 8
  lookalikes (ordinary work in words a filter fears), 12 attacks across injection, secrets and excessive agency,
  and 5 off-topic. Your kit's speakers, your kit's documents and numbers.
- Run **two guards** on it, at least one of them a model (Llama Guard through OpenRouter, gpt-oss-safeguard with your
  own written policy, or a NeMo Guardrails input rail). Report attacks caught, good messages blocked (false
  positives), p50 latency and ₹ per 1,000 messages. Read every miss and every false alarm and say why.
- **One injection through a tool result.** Find a field in your kit that someone outside your company can write
  (a supplier note, an employee's comment, a customer's address). Poison it in a sandbox, run your agent three times,
  and show the code check (your A7 guard) refusing the write. If your guard does not stop it, fix the guard.

### 3. A durable approval that survives a crash
- Your module's approval wait as a Temporal workflow (adapt `durable.py`): the agent turn that saves the document,
  a **signal** for the approver's decision, a **timer** that reminds them, a **read-back** of Bahi before you act on
  the signal.
- The drill: start a Temporal dev server (on Modal or Colab), save the document, **SIGKILL the worker while it waits**,
  send the signal with no worker running, start a new worker. Prove from Bahi that there is exactly one document and
  it ended in the right state. Export the history and replay it with `Replayer` with no server.
- One sentence on what happened to a model call that was in flight when the worker died (look at its attempts).

### 4. Data
Either move your A3 index to pgvector (same vectors, your questions: recall@5 before and after, and the permission
filter as a `WHERE`), or keep your agent's sessions in Redis with an expiry and show a second process reading the
conversation the first one wrote. If you do neither, explain in three lines why your module does not need it yet.

### 5. A budget at the gateway
Put LiteLLM in front of your agent: one virtual key for your module's team with a budget, one fallback model.
Show the request at which the budget refuses, one call answered by the fallback, and the spend per key as LiteLLM
recorded it. Budgets are in US dollars in LiteLLM; keep them tiny for the test.

### 6. Cost per transaction
From your own token logs (A5 and A7 runs, or today's), the ₹ cost of one complete transaction of your module, at the
list prices in `cost.py`, for two models. Then the monthly bill at a volume your kit's README makes plausible, with
the volume marked as an assumption. Show the spread, not only the mean: which run was the most expensive, and why?

### 7. The runbook (one page)
For the person on call: what is monitored and the threshold that pages someone; how to switch the agent off (the
kill switch) and what users see; what to do when the model provider is down (the fallback); how to find a stuck
approval (Temporal UI query); how to rotate a key; who is woken at 2 AM and who decides to switch it back on.

## Guided track and builder track
- **Guided (configuration):** reuse the lecture's scripts with your kit's tool names. Your labelled set, the NeMo
  `prompts.yml` policy, the LiteLLM `config.yaml` and the cost table are configuration; the workflow is `durable.py`
  with your tools in `durable_activities.py`.
- **Builder (Python):** write your own workflow with an extra path (a rejection, an escalation after the second
  reminder), add a `workflow.patched(...)` change and prove that the old history still replays, and add a scanner
  for the outsider-editable fields of your kit's tool results with its own false-positive count.

## Hints
- Run everything heavy on Modal, never on your laptop. The lecture's runner `experiments/l11_modal.py` has three images
  (guard, durable, data) with the pinned versions.
- Derive your workflow id from the document's content, like `ask-rasoi-order-KJOSHI-GSK-SIL-05L-20000`, so starting
  it twice is refused.
- A guard that blocks every attack in your set and 10% of good messages may still be the wrong choice. Say what a false
  alarm costs your users.
