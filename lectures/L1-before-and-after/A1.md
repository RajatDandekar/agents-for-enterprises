# A1 · Your Before, and the After you want

**Agentic Systems Engineering · Lecture 1 · Vizuara · Dr. Rajat Dandekar · Due before Lecture 3 (Sat 7 Nov 2026)**

In Lecture 1 you watched Emma at Northbridge Cycles order 2,000 battery packs. The legacy way took 14 screens, 14 codes, two error codes and a Draft that only looked saved. Ask Northbridge took one sentence per mission and a yes before each save. That story is not really about e-bikes or ERPs. Almost every organisation has a process where people turn what they want into codes, screens and hand-offs, and find out late when it went wrong.

This assignment asks you to find that process in **your own work or domain**, describe it honestly, and say what a good assistant for it would be like. There are no right answers to look up. What we grade is careful observation and clear thinking.

Use a process you know first-hand or can ask someone about. Leave out anything confidential: change names, and never paste real customer, patient or employee data. If you have no work process to use, pick one from daily life that involves an organisation: a visa application, an insurance claim, booking a repair under warranty, or enrolling a child in school.

## Part 1 · Your Before (`BEFORE.md`)

Describe one process from start to finish, the way it is done today.

1. **The job.** Say what gets done, who asks for it, how often it happens, and what record it creates or changes. A record can be an order, a claim, a ticket, a booking, a case file or a payment.
2. **The route.** List every step in order. For each step, say who does it, in which system or tool (an ERP, a portal, a spreadsheet, email, a phone call, paper), and what they must type, choose or remember. Then count:
   - the steps;
   - the systems;
   - the people;
   - the codes and exact names someone must know;
   - the hand-offs between people.

   NB1 exercise 6 gives you a small structure that does this counting.
3. **Where it goes wrong.** Give at least three real failure points. For each one, say what the person sees (the exact message, if there is one) and what they need to know to avoid it. Try to find one of each:
   - a **silent outcome**: it looks done but is not, like Emma's Draft;
   - an **error that does not help**: a code with no explanation, like ERR-PO-0412;
   - a **rule that lives in someone's head**: something only the experienced person knows, like Luis and the UN 38.3 certificate.
4. **The cost.** Estimate how long it takes the first time and when done by someone careful, and how long it waits between people. Say how you estimated it: a timer, a guess, or someone else's account. An honest rough estimate beats a precise invented one.

## Part 2 · The After you want (`AFTER.md`)

Write about the experience you want, not the technology. Answer each question in a short paragraph, with concrete examples from your process.

1. **The sentence.** What would the person say instead of doing the route? Write three to five sentences they might really say. Make at least one of them a question no screen answers today, like "Can we buy from Brightcell instead?"
2. **How should it feel?** Think about what the person sees, how much they type, what they are shown before anything is saved, and how they know it is finished. What would make them trust it after a week? What would make them stop using it?
3. **What must it never do without asking?** List the actions that need a human yes, and say whose yes: the person's, a manager's, or someone with a certain limit. List anything it must never do at all, even if asked.
4. **How would you know it did the right thing?** Name the proof it should give back: a document number, a status, a link, a message to someone. Say how *you* could check it afterwards without trusting its own words. Where would you look?
5. **What happens when it is wrong?** Pick two ways it could fail. One should be a misunderstanding (the wrong supplier, the wrong date, the wrong person). The other should be a system failure (a timeout, or a rule it did not know about). For each, ask: who notices, how soon, how is it undone, and what harm is done in between?
6. **What is still yours?** Say what part of the job should stay with a human, and why.

## Part 4 · Your capstone, a first sketch (`CAPSTONE.md`, half a page)

Over the course you will build an assistant like Ask Northbridge for a process you choose, and demo it in Lecture 12 (Sun 6 Dec). Start it now, in **your own domain**. Your Before from Part 1 is a good candidate.

1. **The process and the people.** Which process, which people speak to the assistant, and which system or systems hold the record.
2. **The sandbox.** You will not connect to a real system. Say what a small fake version of it needs: which records, which rules, and which traps. Ledgerline is our example of such a sandbox.
3. **Five activities.** Name the five things the assistant must be able to do, one line each, for example `check_leave_balance`: "how many days a person has left". These become your tools in Lecture 4.
4. **Two never-events.** Name two things that must never happen, written so they could be tested ("a claim is never paid twice"). They become your test scenarios in Lecture 7.
5. **One open question.** Say what you do not know yet about your process.

If your domain is hard to sandbox, you can base the capstone on one of the module kits in `modules/` (HR leave, payables, sales orders, warranty claims, stock transfers, travel claims, contracts, order amendments). Say why you chose it.
