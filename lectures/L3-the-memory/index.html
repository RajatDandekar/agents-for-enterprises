idf turns “how rare” into a weight

idf(t) = ln(1 + N − df + 0.5df + 0.5)

N is the number of chunks, df the number that contain t.

brightcell in the dump: N = 28,953, df = 6. ln(1 + 28,947.5 / 6.5) = .
order: df = 6,269, idf = .

The same word weighs differently in different collections: idf is about the chunks you search, not about English.

Ten mentions are not ten times better

Term frequency saturates. The weight rises fast, then flattens towards a ceiling set by k1. Drag k1.

weight = idf · tf · (k1 + 1)tf + k1
for a chunk of average length

In D, §6 (chunk 5) says “supplier” times. BM25 counts that as about twice one mention, not ten times.

The same word counts more in a short chunk

Both chunks say “battery” once and “packs” once. BM25 divides by length relative to the average (b = 0.75). Drag b.

Add the pieces: BM25 ranks D’s chunks

Each cell is idf × saturation × length for one token in one chunk. A chunk’s score is the sum of its row.

RRF adds 1 / (60 + rank) from each list

Reciprocal rank fusion ignores the scores and uses only positions. Emma’s question on D, the six best fused chunks.

No model in RRFChunks 12 and 1 tie at 0.032522: ranks (1, 2) and (2, 1) give the same sum. RRF has no LLM and no weights to learn. It is the six lines of rag.rrf, and it cannot read a word of the chunks.

k decides what a top place in one list is worth

Drag k, or switch question. Small k: the top of either list wins. Large k: agreement between the lists wins.

The alternative is weighted score fusion: rescale each list’s scores to 0–1 and add them as α · dense + (1 − α) · BM25, with α tuned on the golden set. The course uses RRF, k = 60 (rag.RRF_K).

Two different things, often confused

Both reorder a list. Only one of them reads the text.

Reciprocal rank fusion

Arithmetic on positions

  • In: two ranked lists. Out: one list.
  • Reads no text. No model, no LLM, nothing trained.
  • Adds 1 / (60 + rank) per list.
  • Cost: a few additions.
The reranker

A cross-encoder: BAAI/bge-reranker-base

  • In: the question and one chunk, together. Out: one relevance number.
  • A transformer with million weights, trained to score pairs.
  • Not a generative LLM: it never writes text, it only scores.
  • Run on the top 20 only.

Twelve rules, in the order we used them

Each one is a step from today, not a slogan.

Audit the corpus: files, shapes, tokens, the mess.
Collect real questions and sort them: genesis, analytical, documentation.
Set metrics and targets with the users, latency included.
Build a golden set from real questions, with gold sources.
Parse by document type; never one reader for all.
Chunk by structure, small, with each document’s header.
Pick an embedding model by measurement (domain models for medicine).
Use BM25 or hybrid search wherever codes matter.
Filter by permission before ranking, and say what was withheld.
Handle versions and effective dates explicitly.
Choose a wiki when synthesis across documents matters and the corpus changes slowly.
Measure again after every change, in CI.

The same method, four domains

What changes is what you must never get wrong.

Pharma

SOPs, batch records, regulatory filings

Versions: only the approved, effective SOP may answer.

Audit trail: every answer logged with the exact document version.

Codes: product and batch numbers need BM25.

Healthcare

Guidelines, formularies, protocols

Permissions: patient data never in the shared index.

Embeddings: a medical model, measured against a general one.

Refuse outside the documents, always.

Banking

Policies, product terms, circulars

Effective dates: the rule in force on the transaction date.

Wiki for products that combine many circulars.

Latency matters at the branch counter.

Legal

Contracts, clauses, precedents

Citations to the clause, checked by script.

Multi-hop is the norm: a wiki of parties and clauses helps.

Confidential matters filtered before ranking.
