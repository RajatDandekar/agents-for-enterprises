# A3 · A memory for your own documents

**Agentic Systems Engineering · Lecture 3 · Vizuara · Dr. Rajat Dandekar · Due before Lecture 5**

In Lecture 3 we gave Ask Northbridge a memory. Henrik shared 28 years of documentation as a repository,
`northbridge-docs`: 693 files, 1,273 PDF pages, 45,557 spreadsheet rows, 4.69 million tokens. We audited it, sorted 304
staff questions into genesis, analytical and documentation questions, and agreed the metrics before building anything:
recall@5, answers, citations, search latency, time to answer, cost, update cost. Pasting everything fitted no model.
We built RAG from scratch and measured 2,112 configurations; the winner was the structured parser, structure-aware
200-token chunks, BM25 and a reranker (recall@5 0.893, search p95 48 ms). We built an LLM Wiki of 683 pages the way
Karpathy's gist describes, queried it by navigation, and linted it (31 of 40 planted problems found). Then we raced them
on 224 questions: the wiki hybrid was most accurate (92.9%), RAG answered in half the time at a fifth of the cost
(89.3%), and RAG shipped behind `search_docs` because latency weighs most.

This assignment asks you to **replicate the class** on Henrik's dump (Parts 1 to 4), then **carry it to your own
domain** (Part 5). We grade measured numbers explained in the lecture's terms, not prose.

Use documents you are allowed to share. Never paste real customer, patient or employee data or internal hostnames. If
you have no documents of your own, use a public corpus you know: a product's public manual, a standards body's
guidelines, a city's bylaws.

**Start from NB3** (`NB3_the_memory.ipynb`, open in Colab from the course page). It clones `northbridge-docs` and runs with
no key; every model call replays. For the parts that need new model calls (your own answers, your wiki's ingest), add a
key in the setup cell (`GEMINI_API_KEY`) and keep the spend under US$3; print it.

## Part 1 · Audit and metrics (`AUDIT.md`) · 2 points

1. **The audit.** For the dump (or your corpus): files, pages, rows and tokens by folder and by class (structured,
   semi-structured, unstructured), with the tokenizer you used. Name three kinds of mess you found, each with a file.
2. **Two parsers.** Show one file where the plain parser loses something the structured parser keeps (NB3 section 2),
   and say what question would fail because of it.
3. **Your metrics.** Write the metrics table for your users: each metric, how you measure it, and a target. Defend the
   latency target in two sentences: who waits, at what point in their work.
4. **Paste-all.** Your corpus's tokens against one model's window, and the input cost of one question if it fitted.

## Part 2 · RAG, tuned (`RAG.md` + your notebook) · 3 points

NB3 sections 5 to 7 are the template.

1. **A golden subset.** 40 questions from `eval/questions.jsonl`, at least three of every documentation type you can
   find, with their gold sources. (In Part 5 you write your own.)
2. **The grid.** Vary at least four knobs: chunker (fixed / structure), chunk size (200 / 400 / 800), BM25 / dense /
   hybrid, the reranker on or off. Use Chroma for the dense part, with `ef_search` set as in `rag.HNSW`. Report recall@5,
   recall@10, MRR and search p50/p95 for every configuration, on the same machine.
3. **Exact codes.** Show one exact-code question where dense search fails and BM25 finds the gold source, with the top 3
   of each.
4. **Pick one** by a rule you state before you look (for example: best recall@5 with search p95 under 200 ms), and say
   which of your targets it meets and which it misses.

## Part 3 · A mini LLM Wiki (`WIKI.md` + a `wiki/` folder) · 3 points

1. **The schema.** Write your `AGENTS.md`: page types and slug rules, the sections each page type has, how a claim cites
   its raw source.
2. **Ingest one folder.** For example `02_procedures/` and `01_policies/` (about 50 files). Produce pages with front
   matter, links and references, an `index.md` and a `log.md`. Report pages, links, references, the model calls, the
   time and the cost.
3. **Query by navigation.** Run three questions: show each chain (index, pages, links) and the answer with citations.
   One of them must be a multi-hop question.
4. **Lint.** Find at least three problems in your wiki or its sources (a contradiction, a superseded document, an
   orphan page) and say how you would fix each.
5. **Update.** Change one source document and re-ingest it. How many pages changed, at what cost? Compare with what RAG
   needs for the same change.

## Part 4 · Your race (`RACE.md`) · 2 points

1. Both methods on your 40 questions, the same answer prompt, the same answer model and judge (NB3 section 8 shows the
   shared answer step). Report answer correct, citation correct, search p95, time to answer p50/p95 and cost per
   question, overall and by question type.
2. **Decide.** Which ships for your users, and why, on the metrics you set in Part 1. Name one question type where the
   loser wins, and say whether a cascade would be worth its latency.

## Part 5 · Your domain (`DOMAIN.md`) · required, ungraded feedback

1. Collect 20 real questions people in your domain ask (rewritten so nothing confidential remains). Sort them into
   genesis, analytical and documentation, and the documentation ones into the lecture's ten types.
2. For 10 documentation questions, write the reference answer and the gold source (document and section).
3. Run your Part 4 winner on them and report recall@5 and answer correct. Which of the twelve rules from Section 10
   mattered most, and which did you break?
