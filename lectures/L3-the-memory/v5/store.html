HNSW: Hierarchical Navigable Small World

Exact search compares the question with all 28,941 vectors. Fine today; slow at ten times the size. HNSW follows links instead.

Small world. Each point links to a few near neighbours, plus a few long links. Any point is then a few hops from any other.

Navigable. A greedy walk: step to whichever neighbour is closer to the question. Stop when none is.

Hierarchical. Sparse upper layers for long jumps, a dense bottom layer for the fine search: motorways, then local roads.

A toy graph of 42 points, searched by the real greedy rule. Chroma’s graph for the dump has layers; our Qdrant collection links each point to others.

Two settings you will meet

m: links per point. More links, a better graph, more memory. Qdrant’s default, on our collection: .

ef: how many candidates the walk keeps as it searches. Larger: closer to exact, and slower.

It can miss: the walk stops where no neighbour is closer, even if a closer vector sits elsewhere.

Qdrant’s own check agrees. ANN Recall tab on our collection: stored points as questions, HNSW at the defaults against exact search. Mean recall@10 ± ; exact ≈ ms, HNSW ≈ ms per search.

Vectors from two models do not mix. Change the model and every chunk must be embedded again.
