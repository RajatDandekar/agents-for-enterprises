## What to build and submit

### Part 1 · Attention by hand (pencil, then numpy) · 3 points
Three tokens from an approval note, with two-number vectors:

| token | query q | key k | value v |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rohit | [1, 0] | [1, 1] | [3, 0] |
| approves | [0, 1] | [0, 2] | [0, 2] |
| PR | [1, 1] | [2, 0] | [1, 1] |

1. With the causal mask, compute for **every** token: the scores q·k for the tokens it may see, the scaled scores
   (÷ √2), the softmax weights, and the output (weighted sum of values). Show the working for "approves" on paper.
2. Check all three rows with numpy (copy the four lines from NB2 part (c)).
3. In two sentences: what happens to the weights of "PR", and why?

### Part 2 · Train the tiny GPT on your module's documents · 4 points
1. In NB2 part (d), replace `text` with your module's documents (`my_text` in the "Your module" cell). Rebuild the
   character vocabulary and train for 1,500 steps (a few minutes on Colab CPU).
2. Report: the number of characters and distinct characters, the loss at step 0 (and the value you expected,
   ln of the vocabulary size), the final train and validation loss, and the training time.
3. Paste three samples at temperature 0.8, each starting with a line typical of your module (for example
   `"Leave request, 26 Oct 2026:"`). Underline every number, name or code in the samples that **looks** right but
   is made up.
4. One paragraph: why is your validation loss higher or lower than Rasoi's (1,19,651 characters of text)? Think about how
   much text you have and how repetitive it is.

### Part 3 · Why the bare model cannot answer your module's questions · 3 points
1. Write three questions your module's users really ask that need **Bahi's live data** (for example, for
   `hr_leave`: "How many casual leave days do I have left?").
2. For one of them, ask a real chat model with no tools (your own key, or any chat app) using the two system
   prompts from the lecture: the modest one and the "you know Rasoi's ..." one. Paste both replies.
3. In your own words (150–250 words), explain why it answers the way it does. Use at least four of these terms
   correctly: token, next-token probability, sampling, temperature, training data, frozen, context window, system
   prompt. End with one sentence on what it would need: the facts (Lecture 3) or the ability to look things up
   (Lecture 4), and which one your question needs.
