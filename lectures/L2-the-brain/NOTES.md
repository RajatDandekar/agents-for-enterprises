## Plate by plate

**1 · Title.** "Last week you saw the After. Today you build its first piece, and we open it up completely."

**2 · Last time, this time.** L1 = the Before (Bahi missions) and the After (Ask Rasoi). Today: the brain only. Third step: you are the engineer; Kavita tries your first build on Monday.

**3 · Monday, 9:12 AM (cold open).** Click through slowly. Read the answer aloud: "We currently have 1,420 units…". Pause. Then the follow-up: it names "our live Pune Stores Inventory Log … this morning's shift". Then the grey Bahi panel: 54,000. Say: "Both the number and the source were invented. By 9 o'clock you will be able to explain every token of that answer." Real run: gemini-3.8-flash, temperature 0, "You know Rasoi's stock" prompt.

**4 · We asked 48 more times.** Two prompts × two models × 12 samples at temperature 1. Click a few chips to read replies (the lite model sometimes writes "Let me check the inventory system…" and then invents). Key facts from our run: the modest prompt on flash declined at temperature 0, but invented a figure in 6 of 12 samples (and 1 of 12 in an earlier run, `cold_open.json`); flash-lite invented in 10 of 12 even with the modest prompt; the "you know the stock" prompt invented in 12 of 12 on both. Nobody said 54,000. Honest note for the room: modern chat models are trained to decline, and often do; the system prompt and chance decide.

**5 · The question for today.** Read the question. Show the nine-part map; tell them we come back to 1,420 in Part 9.

**6 · Part 1.**

**7 · A language model.** Definition first. The surprise: Qwen2.5-0.5B guesses " pipes" (10%), " plates", " vessels"; " cookers" is 5th at 4%. "It has never heard of Rasoi." This sets the theme: plausible, not true.

**8 · Try other Rasoi text.** Click the three prompts. "… before the Diwali" → " season" 30%, " festival" 19%. "… buys its gaskets from" → " a", " the", " China", " India". Ask the room: why not Deccan Polymers?

**9 · Again and again.** Drag the slider. Greedy = always the top token. Ten steps give "… before the new machine can be put into operation. She has". Point: each new token changes the next guess.

**10 · The loop (PaperBanana).** One token per turn. Name the boxes we will open.

**11 · The stock question as a game.** The prompt ends in a space (Qwen writes the space as its own token; in the first run without it the top guess was the space itself, 37%). Digits: "1" 34%, "2" 17%, "5" 14%. Ten samples: 350 boxes, 200 units, 5 tonnes… Greedy gives 1000000. "This is the cold open in miniature."

**12 · Check yourself.** Two questions. Wait for answers.

**13 · Part 2.**

**14 · Letters, words or tokens.** Step through: letters (63), words (8), Qwen tokens (48) for the PO line. Definition of token.

**15 · BPE by hand.** Drag 0 → 10 merges. First merges: e+t, g+a, ga+s, gas+k, gask+et, gasket+_. Vocabulary 9 → 19, corpus 76 → 23 tokens. Real tokenizers: 1,51,665 (Qwen2.5) and 50,257 (GPT-2).

**16 · Real tokenizers.** Click through the four sentences and both tokenizers. Qwen splits every digit (20,000 = 6 tokens + 1 space). " ₹" is one Qwen token, 3 bytes in GPT-2. Hindi: 43 Qwen tokens / 64 GPT-2 vs 20 for the English sentence. GPT-2 is shorter on the PO line (38 vs 48) because it keeps digit groups.

**17 · Why token counts matter.** Context, cost, speed. Kavita's question + system prompt = 77 tokens in, 25 out (Gemini's count). All Rasoi policy docs = 13,144 Qwen tokens.

**18 · The model sees numbers.** Token IDs. "What does it do with a row number?" leads to Part 3.

**20 · Part 3.**

**21 · A token becomes a vector.** The table: 1,51,936 rows × 896 numbers = 136 million numbers (28% of the model). Show the real first 8 numbers of " supplier" (" gasket" is two tokens, " g" + "askets": good aside).

**22 · Meaning as direction.** Cosine definition. Click gasket, supplier, invoice, Diwali, Pune. Bars coloured by group.

**23 · The map.** Opens on vectors after block 8 (the cleanest: nearest word in the same group for 90% of words, vs 65% for raw table rows). Toggle to "raw table rows" and back. Click words. Say what PCA is in one sentence.

**24 · Neighbours in the whole table.** " Pune" → Mumbai, Maharashtra, Kolkata, Chennai… " supplier" → its spellings and 供应商 (Chinese for supplier). "It knows how words are used, not what Rasoi has."

**25 · Check yourself.** Q2 leads into attention.

**26 · Part 4.**

**27 · Query, key, value.** Step through q, k, v columns. Definition last.

**28 · Attention for "gaskets", by hand.** Do it at the board as the steps appear: scores 0, 2, 6, 2; ÷√2; e^x; weights 0.013, 0.052, 0.883, 0.052; output ≈ [0.16, 7.2]: mostly the value of 20,000.

**29 · The causal mask.** Three stages of the same matrix. Why mask: otherwise it would copy the answer during training.

**30 · Real attention in Qwen2.5.** Presets: layer 8 head 5 (the last piece of " gaskets" puts 77% of its weight on the digits of 20,000; layer 0 head 7 puts 100% on the comma inside it); layer 8 head 7 (one token back, 84%); the first-token sink (59% of all weight lands on the first token). Let students call out a layer and head; click rows.

**31 · Many heads.** Drag the layer slider; patterns differ by head and by depth.

**32 · Check yourself.** Q2 is the A2 trick (equal scores → plain average).

**33 · Part 5.**

**34 · One block, built up.** The PaperBanana figure is revealed from the bottom with each step: norm, attention, add (residual stream), MLP, stack ×24, then the top with the real probabilities.

**35 · Position.** Sum ignores order; position vectors (tiny GPT) or rotary positions (Qwen2.5).

**36 · The model by the numbers.** 494 million parameters, 24 blocks, 896 per vector, 14 heads sharing 2 key/value sets, MLP 4,864, vocabulary 1,51,936, context 32,768. Gemini's size is not published: do not guess.

**37 · Follow one token up the stack.** Drag from block 0 to 24. Up to block 19 the best guess is junk or 季 (Chinese for "season"); from block 20 it is " season". A fun aside on multilingual models thinking in another language mid-stack. Say clearly: the logit lens is a view, not how the model is used.

**38 · Logits and softmax.** Four columns appear one by one. Same softmax as in attention.

**39 · Check yourself.**

**40 · Part 6.**

**41 · Loss is surprise.** Toggle the three sentences. " run" costs 14.0 bits (it expected " season"); " elephant" 18.5 bits; a blind guess costs 17.2 bits on every token. Names and numbers are surprising.

**42 · Gradient descent.** Tiny GPT: 8,30,720 numbers, 1,500 steps, 365 s on 2 CPU cores (about 6 min). Loss 4.57 → train 0.23 / validation 0.33. Mention: our corpus is very repetitive, so the loss gets very low.

**43 · What the tiny GPT learnt.** Step 0 gibberish vs step 1,500: "GSK-SIL-05L in Pune stores, 53,000 on hand, using 3,500 a day, 4 days of cover." Point at 53,000 and "4 days" (53,000 / 3,500 is not 4): the format is perfect, the facts are invented. This is the cold open, built by us.

**44 · Three stages of training (PaperBanana).** Pretraining → instruction tuning → preference tuning → frozen. Qwen2.5: 18 trillion pretraining tokens (its technical report).

**45 · Stage 1.** Base-model continuations: greedy stock figure 1000000; "buys its gaskets from a supplier at a price of $100 per unit".

**46 · Stage 2.** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct with the modest prompt: "We currently stock 500 5-litre silicone gaskets in our Pune store." Turn 2 waffles about "inventory records". A small model invents even with the modest prompt.

**47 · Stage 3.** The big model's modest-prompt refusal vs its confident-prompt invention. "The habit to decline is a preference, not a rule." 6 of 12 samples still invented with the modest prompt.

**48 · Frozen at training time.** What it has; what it does not.

**49 · Check yourself.** Q1: ln(82) = 4.41; measured 4.57.

**50 · Part 7.**

**51 · Temperature and top-p.** Live slider demo: T = 0.1 (one token wins), 1, 2; then top-p 0.5. Switch to the stock prompt: the digits flatten as T rises.

**52 · Same prompt, different text.** Real Qwen samples at T = 0, 0.7, 1.0, 1.5 and top-p 0.9. T = 1.5 turns to noise. Tie back to 1,450 / 2,450 / 3,420 from plate 3.

**53 · Check yourself.** Q2 is the key one: temperature 0 makes it invent the same number every time.

**54 · Part 8.**

**55 · Messages are just text.** PaperBanana figure, then the real Qwen chat template (76 tokens).

**56 · The context window.** No memory between calls; the history is re-sent. Gemini 77 → 111 tokens in; Qwen 76 → 112. Qwen2.5-0.5B window: 32,768 tokens.

**57 · Cost and speed.** 77 in / 25 out, 3.9 s, ₹0.01 at the course's assumed list price (₹88/USD, `bahi/llm.py`). Prices are assumed list prices, not a bill.

**58 · Check yourself.** Q2 previews L3.

**59 · Part 9.**

**60 · The cold open, explained.** Four steps, then the PaperBanana gap figure. Make the room say it: "plausible text, no connection to Bahi."

**61 · What would fix it.** L3 knowledge, L4 hands. The real JSON: offered find_item and check_stock, gemini-3.8-flash replied with `{"name": "find_item", "arguments": {"query": "5 litre silicone gasket"}}` and no number. Then the check_stock result: 54,000 in WH-PUN-STR. "A tool call is the model writing JSON; our code does the rest. That is Lecture 4."
