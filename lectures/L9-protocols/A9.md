# A9 · Your module's doors

**Lecture 9 · Protocols and Integration · due before Lecture 12 (demo day)**

In Lecture 9 Rasoi's toolbox opened four doors: hosts like Claude Desktop and VS Code, a sign-in for remote use, a
supplier's own agent over A2A, and n8n. Each door was also a way in for an attacker. Now do the same for **your
module kit** (`modules/<your-kit>/`), and prove that your doors are safe with numbers from your own tables.

You will build four things. Each one copies a piece we built in class: `bahi/bahi/course/protocols/rasoi_mcp.py`,
`deccan.py`, `security.py` and `auth.py`.

## 1 · Serve your module with the official MCP SDK (mcp 2.3.0)
- Serve your kit's tool folders (from A4) with the SDK's low-level `Server`, bound to the signed-in person's own session.
- Add **one resource**: your module's policy document, as `<module>://policy/<name>`. Add a resource template if your
  module has live data worth reading like a file (for example `hr://leave-balance/{employee_id}`).
- Add **one prompt**: the job your module's users do most often, written as reviewed steps with its arguments. Attach the
  policy as an embedded resource, as `raise_a_pr` does.
- **One write must ask the person to confirm** through elicitation (the 2026-07-28 `input_required` and retry), with a
  question that shows the real numbers. Sign `requestState` and refuse a tampered retry.

## 2 · Check it in MCP Inspector (2.12.0)
- Open your server in MCP Inspector over stdio, in the **modern** era (`protocolEra: modern`).
- Submit five screenshots: connection info, the tools list, one tool run, your resource, and your prompt filled in.
- Add a sixth: the elicitation form for your confirmed write.
- Run Inspector's CLI with `--strict` on `tools/list`, and paste the output (it must report no schema problems).

## 3 · A counterparty over A2A (a2a-sdk 1.2.3)
- Pick a company your module deals with that has a mind of its own: a transporter for logistics, a bank for payments, a
  staffing agency for HR, a dealer for sales.
- Publish its **agent card** at `/.well-known/agent-card.json` with at least two skills.
- Give it its **own books**: a small dict of its data and two or three deterministic tools. Your module must never see
  these tables.
- Run **one task** from your module's agent to it and record the sequence with `wire.Recorder`: the card GET, the
  messages, the task states. Use streaming for at least one turn.
- Keep ids and timestamps out of every prompt, so the run replays from its cassette.

## 4 · Red-team your own server
- Write **one attack**: either a poisoned tool description on a toy third-party server, or an injected instruction
  inside a tool result (a document, an email, a quote).
- Run it at least **10 times** (10 phrasings, temperature 0) with no defence. Then add your defence (pinning, a taint
  gate, or a binding or scope fix) and run the same 10 again.
- Judge success **by your module's tables**, never by the model's answer. Report attempted, succeeded and utility (did
  the user still get a correct answer?).
- If your attack never lands, say so, strengthen it once, and log both versions, as we did with quote v1 and v2.

## What to submit
- `server.py` (or your kit's `mcp_server.py`), `counterparty.py`, and `redteam.py`.
- `logs/a9_wire.json`: both eras' wire for your confirmed write, and your tampered-state refusal.
- `logs/a9_a2a.json`: the recorded A2A sequence, with wall time and message counts.
- `logs/a9_redteam.json`: every run, and a table of attempted, succeeded and utility, before and after the defence.
- The six Inspector screenshots, and a one-page `A9_REPORT.md`:
  - what you built;
  - the numbers;
  - one thing that surprised you;
  - one thing a real deployment would add (HTTPS, a real identity provider, token rotation).

## Two tracks
- **Guided:** copy `rasoi_mcp.py` and swap in your folders, documents and prompt text. Copy `deccan.py` and swap the
  books (keep `DeccanBooks`' shape). Copy `security.run_trial`'s structure for your attack.
- **Builder:**
  - Write the counterparty with its own LLM loop and a skill that can end in `input-required` (it asks your agent for
    a missing document number), and trigger it on purpose.
  - Put your server behind `auth.py`'s sign-in with at least two scopes.
  - Show that a read-only token sees only the read-only tools.

## Example: HR · leave
- **Resource:** `hr://policy/leave`. **Template:** `hr://leave-balance/{employee_id}`.
- **Prompt:** `apply_for_leave(from, to, reason)`.
- **Confirmed write:** "Apply for 3 days of earned leave, 4–6 Nov? You have 11 left; it waits for your manager."
- **Counterparty:** "Sahyadri Staffing", whose agent quotes a temporary replacement and a start date from its own roster.
- **Attack:** a CV attachment whose text tells the assistant to approve the leave of the person reading it.
