# A10 · A reliability report for your module

**Set:** end of Lecture 10, Sunday 29 November. **Due:** before Lecture 11, Saturday 5 December, 7 AM IST.
**Feeds the rubric:** operations (10), and the *production stack* line (10) on demo day: observability and evals are
two different layers of the menu, so a complete A10 already gives you both of your two industry tools.

Rasoi's plant head asked three questions about Ask Rasoi. Your module's owner will ask the same about yours:
*What did it do? Does it still follow our rules after a change? Has anyone tried to break them?* You answer with
your kit's agent (`modules/<kit>/`), the tools from today, and **numbers you measured**, on one page.

The library from the lecture is `bahi.course.obs`: `otel.instrument`, `suite` (the DeepEval pieces), `redteam`
(`run_attack`, `broken`), `calibrate` (`confusion`, `report`). The CI workflow is
`lectures/L10-observability-evals/ci/ask-rasoi-evals.yml`; the promptfoo files are in `lectures/L10-observability-evals/redteam/`.

## What to build

1. **Instrument it.** Wrap your kit's agent with `instrument(agent, tracer, conversation_id, user)` (or write your own
   spans) using the GenAI names: `invoke_agent`, `chat`, `execute_tool`, with model, tokens and tool names. Send the
   spans to **Langfuse** (free cloud or self-hosted) **or Arize Phoenix** (`phoenix serve`, no account). Run your four
   scenarios. Submit two screenshots of your own trace (the tree, and one generation opened) and the span JSON
   (`SpanLog`). Decide what content you send: write one sentence on what you kept out and why.
2. **Ten DeepEval tests and a CI gate.** At least ten tests: one per scenario checked by code on Bahi (`BahiState` or
   your own `BaseMetric`), a never-event test, one `ToolCorrectnessMetric` on your main flow, at least one `GEval` with
   written evaluation steps and Bahi's read-back as context, and a cost test. Copy the workflow file and make it run
   your tests. Then write a **deliberately broken prompt** (a plausible "cleanup" pull request, not nonsense) and show
   the gate blocking it: `pytest` exit code 1, which tests failed, and why. Known failures go in an `xfail` list with a
   reason, never deleted.
3. **Red-team it with promptfoo.** Twelve attacks at least: your kit's never-events × at least three delivery
   strategies, and **one indirect injection** planted in your kit's own Bahi data (a name, a remark, a document a tool
   returns). Grade them on Bahi's tables with a Python assertion, never on the reply. Run against two targets (for
   example with and without your A7 trust layer). Report the **attack success rate** per target, and read every hit.
4. **Calibrate one judge.** Run your `GEval` (or the L7 judge with your rubric) on your suite's transcripts and compare
   it with your code checks: the confusion matrix, agreement, chance agreement and **kappa**, computed by you and checked
   with `calibrate.report`. Find the sentence missing from your rubric, add it, run again, and report both kappas.

## Submit

- Your code: the instrumented agent, `test_<kit>.py`, the workflow file, `promptfooconfig.yaml`, your provider and
  assertion, the calibration script.
- `logs/`: the span JSON, the two pytest JSON or XML reports (good prompt, broken prompt), promptfoo's `results.json`,
  the calibration rows. Everything must replay with no key (commit your cassettes).
- `REPORT.md`, **one page**, in this order:
  1. *What it did:* the two screenshots, one sentence each.
  2. *Does it still follow the rules:* tests passed and failed for both prompts, the gate verdict, the known failures.
  3. *Has anyone tried to break it:* ASR per target, the hits read, what stopped the rest (the model or your code).
  4. *Can we trust the tests:* the matrix, both kappas, the rubric sentence you added.
  5. *What you would watch online:* two trace-only checks you would run on live traffic, and why.

## Checklist

- [ ] Spans carry the GenAI names; screenshots are of your own trace, not the lecture's.
- [ ] At least ten DeepEval tests; the gate allows the good prompt and blocks the broken one; exit codes shown.
- [ ] At least twelve promptfoo attacks, one indirect; success read from Bahi; ASR for two targets.
- [ ] A confusion matrix and kappa, before and after one rubric fix.
- [ ] Every number in `REPORT.md` points to a file in `logs/`.
- [ ] No key, password or personal data in any span, log or screenshot.

## How it is graded

| Part | Points | Full marks when |
|---|---|---|
| Instrumentation | 2 of operations (10) | GenAI names on every span; your trace in Langfuse or Phoenix; a content decision stated |
| Tests and gate | 4 of operations | ten tests; the gate blocks a plausible broken prompt and allows the good one; known failures listed with reasons |
| Red-team | 2 of operations | twelve attacks incl. one indirect; Bahi-state assertions; ASR for two targets; every hit read |
| Judge calibration | 2 of operations | matrix, agreement and kappa computed by you; one rubric fix measured |
| Production stack (demo day) | counts toward 10 | Langfuse or Phoenix (observability) and DeepEval or promptfoo (evals) wired in, pinned, with recorded output and a reason in `STACK.md` |

Numbers that did not come from your run score 0. A screenshot of the lecture's trace scores 0.

## Two tracks

- **Guided (configuration).** Use `instrument()`, `bahi.course.obs.suite` and the lecture's promptfoo folder as they
  are; change the scenarios, the never-events and the attack texts to your kit's. Phoenix is enough for step 1.
- **Builder (Python).** Write your spans yourself with the OpenTelemetry SDK (no `instrument()`), add a custom
  `SpanProcessor` that drops message content for one tool you decide is sensitive, run Langfuse's `run_experiment` over
  a dataset of your scenarios for two prompt versions stored in Langfuse prompt management, and add an online evaluator
  that reads your traces back from Langfuse's API and writes a score to each.
