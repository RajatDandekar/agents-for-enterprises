AMD-SUP-2026-02 is Amendment 2 to the [[pol-sup-003|Supplier Policy]], issued by the Purchase department and effective from 1 September 2026 [^r1][^r2]. It tightens compliance acceptance for lithium-cell components by replacing clause §4.4 of [[pol-sup-003|POL-SUP-003]] [^r2][^r5]. As of 26 October 2026, [[topic-un-38-3-certificates|UN 38.3 certificates]] must be issued within the last 12 months and maintain a valid-to date at least 60 days beyond the purchase order date [^r2].

### §1. What changes
With effect from 1 September 2026, AMD-SUP-2026-02 replaces [[pol-sup-003|POL-SUP-003]] §4.4 with a requirement that a [[topic-un-38-3-certificates|UN 38.3 certificate]] is accepted only if issued within the last 12 months and with a valid-to date at least 60 days after the purchase order placement date [^r2]. Certificates meeting the former standard of issue within 24 months and non-expiry are treated as having no certificate for new orders placed from 1 September 2026 onward, while orders released prior to that date are unaffected [^r3]. All other provisions of [[pol-sup-003|POL-SUP-003]] §4 remain fully in force [^r7].

### §2. Why
The revision followed a July 2026 incident where a [[items-bat|battery pack]] lot carrying a 22-month-old test report failed incoming abuse testing after an unannounced change of cell maker [^r4]. The amendment was commissioned following the 30 July 2026 review to enforce a 12-month validity window and a 60-day post-order margin on compliance documentation [^r9].

### §3. What purchase staff must do
Before raising a purchase order for any lithium-cell component, purchase staff must verify the certificate's issue date and valid-to date against the criteria in AMD-SUP-2026-02 [^r5]. In accordance with [[sop-pur-005|SOP-PUR-005]], staff may not raise a new purchase order for [[items-bat|battery packs]] if fewer than 60 days remain before [[topic-un-38-3-certificates|UN 38.3 certificate]] expiry without an approved new test report [^r8]. A [[topic-un-38-3-certificates|UN 38.3 test report]] must be less than 12 months old and remain valid for at least 60 days following the order date, as detailed in [[sop-pur-001|SOP-PUR-001]] [^r11]. On the first working day of every month, the senior buyer must run the certificate-expiry report described in [[man-pur-72|MAN-PUR-72]] §22.6 with a 90-day window and contact all suppliers whose [[topic-un-38-3-certificates|UN 38.3 certificates]] expire within that period [^r6]. The 12-month and 60-day certificate rules are not validated automatically by Ledgerline's optional PUR.CERT_CHK parameter [^r10].

## Versions and conflicts
- **Current rule (from 2026-09-01)**: A UN 38.3 certificate is valid only if issued within the last 12 months and its expiry date is at least 60 days after the purchase order placement date [^r2].
- **Older rule**: [[pol-sup-003|POL-SUP-003]] §4.4 accepted certificates issued within 24 months provided they were not expired [^r3].
- **Kind**: superseded. Certificates conforming solely to the old 24-month rule count as having no certificate for orders placed on or after 1 September 2026, though orders released before that date remain valid [^r3].
