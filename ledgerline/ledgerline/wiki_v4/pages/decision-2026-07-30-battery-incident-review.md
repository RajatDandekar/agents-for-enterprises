Meeting decision from the battery incident review held on 30 July 2026 following incoming abuse test failures at the plant. It mandated immediate quarantine of the rejected battery lot and initiated an operational update to purchasing rules. This decision directly prompted the drafting of [[amd-sup-2026-02|Amendment 2 to the Supplier Policy]] to enforce strict certificate age limits during intake.

## Incident background

On 28 July 2026, a lot of battery packs failed the incoming abuse test during routine verification at the plant [^r1]. Subsequent examination of the compliance file revealed that the supplier's test report was 22 months old and that the cell maker had changed without notification to purchasing or fresh test documentation [^r2]. Stores had accepted the intake paperwork because standard receipt routines only verified certificate presence against the purchase order rather than checking document age [^r3].

## Agreed actions

[[user-lmoreno|Luis Moreno]] was tasked with drafting [[amd-sup-2026-02|Amendment 2]] to [[pol-sup-003|POL-SUP-003]] to introduce a 12-month document window and a 60-day margin [^r4].

[[user-pjansen|Pieter Jansen]] was assigned to maintain the failed lot in quarantine in the holding bay until the supplier arranges collection back to their own depot [^r5].
