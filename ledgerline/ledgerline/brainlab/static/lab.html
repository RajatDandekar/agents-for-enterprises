Step 1 · Your Northbridge badge

Four things only you know.

Today you work at Northbridge Cycles. This badge holds four facts about you: your stores and their stock, the e-bike you designed, your team and your badge number. They exist nowhere else: not on the internet, not in any training data. The brain has never seen them.

Meet the brain

A GPU is a chip with thousands of small cores that do the model's matrix multiplications in parallel. This class has its own GPUs in Modal's cloud (US East); your browser only sends text. No tools, no Ledgerline: whatever it says comes only from its weights and its prompt, which is Ask Northbridge's own system prompt.

Step 3 · Teach it

Write what it should know.

We filled in your badge's facts and Emma's stock line. Change them, add your own (an invented product, a team, a number), or delete some. 2 to 5 facts. For each one, write the question you will ask and the short answer it must say.

Step 4 · Two ways to give it the facts

Put them in the prompt, or train them into the weights.

Same model, same facts, same questions. Plus some it was never taught: each question asked a different way, a store you never mentioned (Leeds), and a general one (France).

A · In the prompt

Paste the facts into the system prompt

No training. The facts ride along with every question, so every question costs more tokens.

B · In the weights

Train it on the facts (a LoRA fine-tune)

Gradient descent on small extra matrices beside every layer. Then we ask with no facts in the prompt: anything it gets right comes from its weights.
