# A1 · Your module, before

**Lecture 1 · The Form and Its Nine Jobs** · due before Lecture 2 (Sun 1 Nov, 7 AM IST)

In class we measured Kavita's gasket order done by hand: 8 screens opened (7 different), 17 fields typed, 8 of them codes, 2 sign-ins, 1 error code, and an order that the status bar called `SAVED` while it sat in Draft. Then we gave a model every service as a tool and watched it fail. A1 does the first half for **your** module: you measure its before, so that every later assignment has something to beat.

## 2. Do its main task by hand

Run Bahi with your module installed and do the after sentence the before way, screen by screen.

```bash
python -m bahi.server --modules hr_leave        # legacy client at http://127.0.0.1:8077/legacy
```

Sign in as the person who speaks (passwords are in `bahi/bahi/modules/_kit.py` and `erp/seed.py`; Kavita is `KJOSHI` / `kavita@123`). Your module's screens appear in the menu tree under their own heading. `View → Service log` shows every call the client made. `modules/<name>/legacy.py` (`happy_path`) lists the screens in the order a careful user opens them; try it yourself first.

Then trigger the **silent outcome** in your module's brief: the case where Bahi says it saved and the work never happens.

## 3. What to submit

One file, `A1_<module>.md` (template below), plus your counts as JSON if you took the builder track.

1. **The before story**, with counts from your run: screens opened, fields typed, codes typed, sign-ins, error codes, service calls, and your own minutes (timed with a stopwatch; say so).
2. **The silent outcome**: what the screen said, what the status field said, what the database holds, and when someone would find out.
3. **What the user carries in their head**: at least four things the screens never tell them (like Kavita's "Fetch before Save" and "Gujarat means -INTER").
4. **The after**: the one sentence, and what the agent should say back in the silent-outcome case.
5. **The nine-jobs table** for your module: for each job, who or what does it on the form today, and what an agent would need instead.
6. **Requirements as numbers**: your pass bar on the module's four scenarios, the never-events that fail a scenario outright, a latency budget, and a ₹ per resolved request.

## 4. Two tracks

**Guided track (configuration).** Use the legacy client. Count by hand as you go: every screen you open, every field you type (mark the ones that are codes), every sign-in, every error code. The Service log gives you the service calls. Fill the template.

**Builder track (Python).** Drive the screens with the same driver that measured Kavita's order, and log the counts:

```python
from fastapi.testclient import TestClient
from bahi import ERP
from bahi.server import create_app
from bahi.legacy_ui.driver import Desk, Tally

MOD = "hr_leave"
app = create_app(lambda: ERP(modules=[MOD]))
http, tally = TestClient(app), Tally()
kavita = Desk(http, tally, "Kavita", modules=[MOD]).sign_in("KJOSHI", "kavita@123")
s = kavita.open("HR-LV-01").press("Find Emp")
...
tally.table()            # your counts
app.state.tenants["default"].live_run()   # every call, with screen, button and stored procedures
```

`lectures/L1-the-form/experiments/a1_example.py` is a complete worked run on `hr_leave` (5 screens opened, 12 fields typed, 3 codes, 2 sign-ins, 8 service calls; log in `experiments/logs/a1_example_hr_leave.json`). Pick a different module, or go further on this one: its silent outcome is not in the example.

Builder track also: run `bahi.agent.naive.NaiveAgent` on one of your module's four scenarios (`bahi.modules._kit.run_module_scenario`) and report what happened. It needs a key, or say that you did not run it.

## 6. How it is graded

A1 is marked **complete** or **redo** and is the base of the capstone's /100. It does not score points by itself; it sets the targets the later rubric lines are measured against:

| Rubric line (/100) | What A1 fixes for it |
|---|---|
| Scenario pass rate (25) | your pass bar and never-events |
| Tools (20) | the screens, services and codes you counted are what your tools must hide |
| Route and authority (15) | jobs 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 in your table |
| Retrieval (15) | job 4: which rules live in documents and people, not the database |
| Operations (15) | your latency budget and ₹ per resolved request; job 8 |
| Demo-day change (10) | your before story is what you show first on 22 November |

A **redo** is given for: counts that are not from a run, a missing silent outcome, or a nine-jobs table with empty rows.
